# Challenge proposed requirements against supplied task outcomes

Save a complete condition set, then follow **Challenge requirements against supplied outcomes**. The new receiver retains every original practice round and condition. Supply an acceptable, unacceptable or unknown task-outcome judgment for each round; every known judgment needs a basis. These are supplied accounts, not authenticated physical results. Unknown judgments provide no vote.

The receiver enumerates every nonempty conjunction of the same declared requirements. An optional, explicit checkbox adds the original complete-task point score as one further requirement. Every comparison reuses the maintained typed evaluators and original point scoring. It does not adjust placements, tolerances, source geometry or task judgments.

An acceptable judgment contradicts a conjunction when any selected requirement is outside tolerance. An unacceptable judgment contradicts it when every selected requirement is within. Missing or numerically unresolved evidence remains unresolved unless a selected outside requirement determines that the conjunction fails. Consistency claims require both acceptable and unacceptable supplied judgments. Unknown rounds stay visible without contributing agreement.

Every hypothesis remains inspectable, including exact contradicting and unresolved rounds and the original criterion statuses and bases. Inclusion-minimal consistent combinations are those with no smaller consistent subset. This finite consistency does not establish causal necessity, generalization, physical validity or learning. Criteria and judgments may have been supplied after seeing outcomes.

An authored control has a correct layout, a shortened arm and a translated layout. Relative arm length can explain the first two supplied outcomes. If the translated layout is judged unacceptable, every relative length/guide conjunction conflicts with those judgments because both relationships remain within tolerance. Including the original point score permits an explanation of the positional failure. A missing endpoint never counts as confirming a judgment.
