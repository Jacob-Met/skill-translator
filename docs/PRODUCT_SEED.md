# SKILL TRANSLATOR product seed

## Purpose

Help people understand, practice, preserve, and adapt skilled physical work. Turn a demonstration into an investigable account of what the expert is trying to achieve, which relationships must hold, what feedback guides their decisions, and how the task can succeed under different conditions.

The ambition is transferable practical knowledge across people, tools, materials, and possibly machines. This is not a video summarizer, pose-scoring exercise, or promise of instant mastery. A learner should gain the ability to judge a situation and act independently. An expert should be able to preserve and communicate knowledge that ordinarily requires years of watching closely and asking the right question.

## The actual experience we want to make possible

A master bookbinder demonstrates an operation. A learner is left-handed, has a differently sized workpiece, and uses another tool. Instead of aligning their wrists with a ghost of the expert's wrists, SKILL TRANSLATOR reveals the intended relationship between the material, tool, support, and resulting shape. They can inspect an important moment, compare before and after, and ask, “What must stay the same if I do this differently?”

Some information is visible; some is not. The expert may be responding to resistance, a sound, or a change in texture that the camera does not capture. The system can ask the expert to describe the cue, demonstrate a useful variation, or provide an appropriate measurement. It should not manufacture a force reading from an ordinary video.

The learner tries their adaptation. Feedback concerns the task's state and result: an alignment was lost, an intended contact never occurred, or the material did not reach the expected condition. They can inspect the relevant evidence, choose an explanation, and decide whether to retry or recover. Assistance can be visual, spoken, tactile where supported, or deferred until a natural pause; constant interruption is not inherently better coaching.

Later, they encounter an unfamiliar variation without the guide open. Succeeding—or recognizing when they need help—is a more important outcome than reproducing the original movement beautifully.

## Abilities that could grow

These are illustrative possibilities, not milestones or the limits of the product.

- **An expert's capture studio.** Record demonstrations, annotate meaningful object states, identify decisions and sensory cues, and compare several successful approaches. Ask targeted questions about what changed rather than requiring an expert to write a complete manual first. Preserve authorship, context, and the right to correct an interpretation.
- **A manipulable explanation of a task.** Explore goals, prerequisites, contacts, tolerances, and completion conditions. Move between a whole workflow and a critical moment while retaining the original demonstration. Ask why a step matters or what another valid route would require.
- **Adaptation to the learner.** Accommodate deliberately supplied differences in handedness, reach, tools, workspace, experience, and preferred feedback. Help discover feasible alternatives instead of grading everyone against one canonical body or technique. Do not infer a person's medical condition from their movement.
- **Recovery as part of expertise.** Learn how experts notice a developing problem, distinguish recoverable states, and choose a sensible next action. A mistake can expose a hidden condition that a flawless demonstration never revealed.
- **Practice that develops judgment.** Compare outcomes, explain tradeoffs, and gradually reduce assistance when appropriate. Let the learner request detail, challenge advice, and revisit uncertain moments. Teaching strategies should adapt without turning practice into permanent remote control.
- **Transfer beyond the original embodiment.** A simulated or physical robot might use the same task relationships while choosing movements suitable for its own joints, gripper, and sensors. A shared goal does not imply shared motion or a policy that automatically transfers from simulation to hardware.

Rare crafts, maintenance knowledge, assembly, creative fabrication, and other uses may reveal valuable directions beyond these examples. Different communities may want tools for teaching, preserving, adapting, or inventing skills—not one universal lesson format.

## The hard transformation

The central question is which parts of a demonstration are essential to its outcome and which are incidental. A repeated movement may reflect a physical constraint, an expert's preference, or an unseen property of the material. The same visible action can succeed or fail for different reasons.

Useful research could connect object identity, changing geometry, temporal task structure, contact relationships, material state, and outcome constraints. Alternative representations should remain possible. Video can reveal some consequences of contact but often cannot determine force, friction, stiffness, or tactile judgment uniquely. Instrumented examples, expert corrections, and carefully chosen variations can supply missing evidence.

Adaptation requires more than recognizing steps. The system must consider whether a different arrangement can satisfy the same functional constraints and what new failure modes it introduces. Coaching adds another challenge: choosing information that helps learning rather than simply maximizing immediate assisted success. No universal framework or full physical simulator is a prerequisite to investigating these problems.

## How knowledge and algorithms can compound

Successful transfers test proposed invariants. Failed transfers expose hidden conditions. Over time, skills could become composable knowledge with meaningful prerequisites, outcome checks, and recovery options, while retaining examples and disagreements. Expert corrections should improve later explanations; learner outcomes should improve teaching without erasing individual preferences.

Before duplicating perception, geometry, simulation, or interaction algorithms, inspect existing estate modules and their owners. Reuse or improve qualified capabilities through clear interfaces and actual consumer tests. Share an algorithm when its contract fits; do not force every product into a common platform or mistake a similar name for equivalent behavior. Independent experiments can proceed in parallel, while dependent source changes and merges need coordination.

## Evidence of meaningful progress

Evaluate functional outcomes on unfamiliar tools, workpieces, and starting states—not pose similarity alone. Can someone adapt a technique, notice a consequential error, recover, and perform later without coaching? Compare with an ordinary demonstration or other relevant teaching baseline, and include expert assessment where the result demands it.

Retain real demonstration and result evidence with appropriate rights and consent. Distinguish authored examples, simulations, physical execution, and actual learning studies. Useful progress may be a single hidden constraint correctly exposed, a successful adaptation the expert never demonstrated, or a recovery that prevents wasted work. Assistance that looks impressive but leaves the learner less independent is a reason to rethink it.

## Nebius and NVIDIA context

The [official Nebius × NVIDIA rules](https://nebiusglobalaihackathon.devpost.com/rules) require actual Nebius runtime use and at least one NVIDIA open-source model. Select substantive roles in the working product and show what those components contribute; a dependency declaration or architecture diagram is not integration evidence. Appropriate model inference, simulation, or evaluation may benefit from cloud execution without placing every coaching interaction behind a slow remote call.

No deployment, hardware operation, clinical use, or submission is established or authorized by this seed. Keep technical qualification, human benefit, and competition eligibility distinct. The product should grow through useful invention, not stop at whichever demonstration first proves feasible.
