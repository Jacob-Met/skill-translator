# Plan a bounded fixed sequence of repeated cue readings

From the withdrawal inquiry, save its original inputs and follow **Plan fixed repeated cue readings from saved original inputs**. Open that saved history or an original v1/v2 cue bundle. Declare the maximum hypothetical future readings (0–6) and a planning basis, alongside the existing total mismatch bound. Inspect to see a minimum-length fixed schedule within this declared family, or an explicit reason no such guarantee is available. Save original inputs retains the original source, complete event history and planning assumptions; reopening recomputes.

Each represented example keeps only its remaining total allowance after disagreements with active past readings. Current transcript revisions count; withdrawn readings remain in history but do not consume active allowance. Nothing resets the total error budget for future readings. Unknown source values supply no separating distance. Unknown decisions prevent a represented-decision guarantee, and an already incompatible active account receives no proposed schedule.

For each pair of different represented decisions, the schedule must contain more known unequal cue positions than the sum of their remaining allowances. Otherwise one possible future answer sequence can still fit both. Count vectors are enumerated in increasing total length; the first feasible vector is a minimum in this fixed family. A deterministic tie selects one vector without claiming that cue is physically better. This is not an adaptive or effort-optimal policy. Failure within six readings does not prove a longer or adaptive policy impossible.

The guarantee is conditional: at least one retained example must fit the complete answer sequence. Unexpected answer sequences may fit none and remain outside evidence. Repeated readings do not establish independent errors, measured reliability, physical success or teaching efficacy. The proposed sequence is a hypothetical inquiry, not an instruction to perform a physical operation.
